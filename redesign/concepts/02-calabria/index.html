1943Chapter I · Catanzaro

A shop, one coffee machine and a city that runs on the bar.

In Catanzaro the day runs on the bar. A quick "un caffè" at the counter before work, another after lunch, one more when a friend walks in. Guglielmo grew up inside that ritual: Guglielmo Papaleo opened his shop on Corso Mazzini with a single coffee machine, and by 1950 the first Guglielmo bar was pouring espresso for the city.

Every blend we import still answers to that counter: short, intense, generous with crema, gone in three sips.

1972Chapter II · Copanello

La torrefazione

The roastery on the Ionian coast.

Guglielmo roasts at Copanello di Stalettì, on the Gulf of Squillace, where the plant has overlooked the Ionian Sea since 1972. Sacks of green coffee from Brazil, Central America, Africa and Asia are cleaned and tasted before they go near a roaster.

Each origin is roasted on its own, up to about 300 °C, until its oils and aroma open, and only then blended into Extra Bar, Classico or Club 99.

Inside the roastery

Four phases, in order

  1. Clean and taste

    Green coffee is cleared of leaves, twigs and stones, then checked by specialists for its organoleptic quality.

  2. Roast each origin alone

    Drum roasters reach up to about 300 °C, with the roast time set for each origin.

  3. Blend after roasting

    The roasted Arabica and Robusta origins are combined into each recipe.

  4. Grind and pack

    Vacuum bricks, valve pouches in a protective atmosphere, tins, capsules, E.S.E. pods and whole beans.

2000Chapter III · Boston

From Corso Mazzini to Hanover Street.

In 2000 Mimmo (Domenico) Elia began importing Caffè Guglielmo to Boston, bringing a Calabrian espresso to the restaurants, pastry shops and families of New England. The small import company grew into L'Espresso USA: distributors in several states, Canada since 2003, and a service team that installs, repairs and lends machines.

Today Guglielmo is poured at 76 cafés, pastry shops, restaurants and markets in Massachusetts and Maine, two dozen of them in the North End alone.
