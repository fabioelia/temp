### Production

- Four phases [S20, S2]: (1) green coffee cleaned of leaves, twigs and stones and evaluated by specialists for organoleptic quality; (2) roasting at up to about **300 °C**, with roast time set for each origin; (3) **blending of the roasted origins** (Arabica and Robusta) into each recipe; (4) grinding and packing - vacuum bricks, one-way-valve pouches in protective atmosphere (Bar 5 Stelle), tins, capsules, ESE pods and whole beans.
- Roasting method: origins roasted separately, blended after roasting [S2, S20]. Wikipedia adds methane-fired rotating-drum roasters, about 240-300 °C for 15-20 minutes, air cooling, computer-controlled dosing (UNVERIFIED) [S7].
- Plant upgrades since 1997 (UNVERIFIED, Wikipedia [S7]): two new roasters; an 18 m outdoor green-coffee silo with 14 cells (about 3,300 sacks); a new green-bean cleaning machine; a roasted-coffee silo.
- Green-coffee origins named by the company: Brazil, Central America, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Vietnam, among others (official e-shop via search index [S1]).

> The house blend of the Southern Italian espresso bar. Extra Bar pairs Arabica with Robusta for a dense crema, an intense aroma and a bold, cocoa-dark finish that stands up to milk. ### 5 Stelle (Bar 5 Stelle)

## 4. Brand voice

1. **Rooted, not nostalgic.** Speak from real places and objects - Catanzaro, Copanello, the bar counter, the moka - instead of generic "old world" imagery.
2. **Short and strong, like the espresso.** Direct sentences, active verbs, no hype words (no "unforgettable", "world's best", "artisanal" without proof).
3. **Warm hospitality.** The welcome of a Calabrian bar: inclusive, generous, a little playful. Italian words sparingly, always clear from context.
4. **Expert, never precious.** Explain roast, grind and crema in plain English; give operators practical, specific guidance.
5. **Honest and exact.** Only verifiable claims; name certifications and blends precisely; label compatibility claims and trademarks correctly.
6. **Two voices, one family.** Guglielmo speaks heritage and taste; Guglielmo USA speaks service and reliability (3-day delivery, loaner machines, on-site techs).
7. **Aim with a wink.** Use the "fa centro" / bullseye idea as a light motif, not in every headline.

**Words we like:** counter, cup, crema, moka, roast, Calabria, Copanello, bar, pour, aroma, target, family, service, delivered.

**Words we avoid:** unforgettable, world-class, best-in-the-world, artisanal (unless proven), authentic (overused - show it instead), "Italian" as a lazy adjective.

**Italian glossary (use sparingly):** *caffè* (coffee / espresso), *un caffè* (an espresso), *miscela* (blend), *torrefazione* (roastery), *crema*, *moka* (stovetop pot), *tazzina* (espresso cup), *il caffè che fa centro* (the coffee that hits the bullseye).

## 5. Calabria / origin story blocks

### Il bar, alla calabrese

In Catanzaro the day runs on the bar. A quick "un caffè" at the counter before work, another after lunch, one more when a friend walks in. Guglielmo grew up inside that ritual: Guglielmo Papaleo opened his shop on Corso Mazzini with a single coffee machine, and by 1950 the first Guglielmo bar was pouring espresso for the city. Every blend we import still answers to that counter: short, intense, generous with crema, gone in three sips.

### The roastery on the Ionian coast

Guglielmo roasts at Copanello di Stalettì, a few kilometers from Catanzaro, where the plant has overlooked the Ionian Sea since 1972. Sacks of green coffee from Brazil, Central America, Africa and Asia are cleaned and tasted before they go near a roaster. Each origin is roasted on its own, up to about 300 °C, until its oils and aroma open, and only then blended into Extra Bar, Classico or Club 99. The plant spans 10,000 square meters.

### From Corso Mazzini to Boston

In 2000 Mimmo (Domenico) Elia began importing Caffè Guglielmo to Boston, bringing a Calabrian espresso to the restaurants, pastry shops and families of New England. What started as a small import company grew into L'Espresso USA: distributors in several states, Canada since 2003, and a service team that installs, repairs and lends machines. Today the same blends poured in Catanzaro reach counters from the North End to Belmont. The coffee comes from Copanello; the delivery, the training and the service calls come from Boston.

Fact basis: Corso Mazzini shop and first bar in 1950 [S1, S6]; Copanello plant since 1972, 10,000 m2, origins roasted separately at up to about 300 °C [S2, S6, S20]; L'Espresso USA founded 2000 in Boston, Canada 2003, service offer [S19, S22]; North End and Belmont accounts from our homepage.

## 7. Brasilena (short)

Brasilena is what Calabria drinks when it is too late for an espresso and too early for an aperitivo. Born as a "gazzosa al caffè" in a 1930s village shop in Girifalco, it is still made by the Cristofaro family's Acqua Calabria: real coffee infused in Calabrian mineral water, lightly sparkling, bittersweet and served ice-cold. US tagline used on our site: "Brasilena, it sparkles your life."

- **Maker:** Acqua Calabria (Cristofaro family), plant at Monte Covello, Girifalco (province of Catanzaro). Not a Guglielmo product; imported and sold by L'Espresso USA.
- Sparkling soft drink made from an infusion of roasted coffee in Calabrian oligomineral water (Acqua Calabria); ingredients: water, sugar, coffee infusion (12%), carbon dioxide, flavorings (Cookist).
- Origins in the 1930s, when the Cristofaro grandfather made a "gazzosa al caffè" in his small emporium in Girifalco, roasting the coffee himself (Gambero Rosso interview, via CalabriaMagnifica / Radio Food).
- The Brasilena name was registered and industrial bottling began in the 1960s (Salvatore Cristofaro); the drink became a regional success in the 1980s; production at Monte Covello since 1982 (sources vary - UNVERIFIED exact years).
- Formats: glass bottles of 18, 25 and 92 cl and a 25 cl slim can; US: 250 ml cans (single, 6, 24) and bottles (24).
- Reported as Calabria's best-selling soft drink and recognized as a "Marchio Storico di Interesse Nazionale" in 2021 (Cookist / Radio Food - UNVERIFIED).
- The vintage label features a smiling woman in a sombrero (illustration signed "R. Casaro" on the carton); the name is said to evoke South American coffee arriving in Calabrian ports (UNVERIFIED).

Sources: S25-S28.
