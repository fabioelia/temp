## 4. Sizing and pyramiding
- **Positions:** at most 3, and energy counts as one theme. Start with XLE $350, COP $300 and the 2x sleeve $250, about 45% invested. VLO is added only on its trigger.
- **Risk per trade:** 1% of equity (~$20), so size = $20 ÷ stop%. Cap any name at 20% and the energy theme at 50%.
- **Pyramid** only when the starter is up 3% and makes a new closing high. Add half the starter size, then move the whole position's stop to breakeven. Never average down.
- **Cuts:**
  - Hard stop at -6/-7% on a close.
  - Halve energy if WTI closes under about $95 for two sessions (it was about $95 earlier this week).
  - Time stop: exit after 8-10 sessions flat.
- **Profits:** take half at +10%, trail the rest under the 10-day low, and sell climax days.
- **Dry powder:** keep at least 50% cash for a real Nasdaq FTD. Beaten AI leaders then bounce hardest, but only after confirmation.

**Evidence vs folklore** (from memory, not searched):
- **Evidence:**
  - Cross-sectional momentum (Jegadeesh-Titman 1993).
  - The 52-week-high effect (George-Hwang 2004).
  - Industry momentum (Moskowitz-Grinblatt 1999).
  - Time-series trend following (Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen 2012).
  - Momentum crashes in sharp rebounds (Daniel-Moskowitz 2016), with stop-losses mitigating them (Han-Zhou-Zhu 2016).
  - Volatility-scaled sizing (Turtle ATR units; Moreira-Muir 2017) and fixed-fractional risk.
- **Folklore:** the magic 7-8% number (it is just a risk budget), cup-with-handle geometry, the exact "Day 4" FTD (small samples, fails often), pyramid ratios, and Livermore anecdotes. "Never average down" is sound ruin math, though.
